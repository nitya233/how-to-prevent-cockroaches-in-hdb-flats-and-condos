How to prevent cockroaches in HDB flats and condos

Preventing cockroaches in HDB flats and condominiums means removing the conditions that let them settle in. Look for cracks, gaps around cabinet hinges, appliance gaps, skirting edges and drain perimeters, because these are common hiding and entry points. Keep food stored, clean up spills, reduce moisture and check wall voids and outdoor areas. Regular inspection of high-risk spots helps you act before a mild problem becomes a larger infestation. Prevention is practical, but it works best when paired with species-aware treatment if cockroaches are already present.

Start with the kitchen and bathroom, where food, warmth and moisture are easiest to find. Check cabinet hinges, cracks, gaps around appliances, skirting boards and drain perimeters. Also look behind wall voids and in outdoor or drain areas. German cockroaches often favour indoor harbourage near appliances, while American and Oriental cockroaches may be associated with drains and moisture. Brown-banded cockroaches can shelter in warmer, higher spots. Seal or treat entry and hiding points, store food in closed containers, fix leaks, clear food debris and keep drains clean. A written report after an inspection can help you track what was found and what was treated.

Prevention should be regular. Inspect high-risk areas and avoid leaving water and food sources available overnight. If you see droppings, egg cases, live insects or grease marks, arrange an inspection rather than waiting. Treatment may include gel baiting in cabinet hinges, cracks and appliance gaps, residual insecticide around skirting, entry points and drain perimeters, insecticidal dust in wall voids, or granular bait for outdoor and drain areas. Mild to moderate infestations usually need 1 to 2 visits, while severe ones need 2 to 3 visits over 3 to 4 weeks. Common questions

Where should I check first for cockroach activity?

Start with kitchen and bathroom high-risk areas. Look at cabinet hinges, cracks, appliance gaps, skirting, drain perimeters, wall voids and outdoor or drain areas. Droppings, egg cases, live insects or grease marks suggest activity. Arrange an inspection if you find signs.

Can prevention alone stop an existing infestation?

Prevention removes conditions that let cockroaches settle in, but an existing infestation may need treatment. The method is chosen by species and may include gel baiting, residual insecticide, insecticidal dust or granular bait. Mild to moderate cases usually need 1 to 2 visits; severe cases need 2 to 3 visits.

What should I do after treatment if cockroaches appear again?

Re-entry is safe 2 hours after treatment, and treated surfaces should not be mopped for 48 hours. If cockroaches return between visits on a bi-monthly plan, free re-treatment is included. Otherwise, arrange an inspection so hiding and entry points can be reviewed and the method checked.
