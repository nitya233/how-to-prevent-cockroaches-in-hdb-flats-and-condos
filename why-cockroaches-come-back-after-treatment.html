Why cockroaches come back after treatment

Cockroaches can return after treatment for several practical reasons. Some hiding or entry points may have been missed, especially in cabinet hinges, cracks, appliance gaps, wall voids, skirting edges and drain perimeters. The species also matters, because American, German, brown-banded and Oriental cockroaches behave differently. Re-entry is safe 2 hours after treatment, but treated surfaces should not be mopped for 48 hours. Mild to moderate infestations usually need 1 to 2 visits, while severe ones need 2 to 3 visits over 3 to 4 weeks. A return visit is not automatically a failed treatment.

Treatment works only where the product reaches the cockroach or its harbourage. If gel bait is placed in cabinet hinges, cracks and appliance gaps, but other hiding points remain untreated, some insects can survive. Residual insecticide around skirting, entry points and drain perimeters may be weakened if surfaces are mopped too soon; treated surfaces should not be mopped for 48 hours. Insecticidal dust in wall voids and granular bait for outdoor and drain areas also need to be placed in the right locations. Some species shelter differently, so a method chosen for one species may not suit another. Missed entry points can allow new cockroaches to move in.

Infestation severity affects timing too. Mild to moderate infestations usually need 1 to 2 visits, while severe ones need 2 to 3 visits over 3 to 4 weeks. If a follow-up is skipped, survivors may breed again. Food, moisture and clutter can support reinfestation, and cockroaches can re-enter from drain areas or neighbouring spaces. On a bi-monthly plan, free re-treatment is included if cockroaches return between visits. If activity continues, arrange an inspection so high-risk areas and written report findings can be reviewed. Common questions

Does one treatment always solve a cockroach problem?

Not always. Mild to moderate infestations usually need 1 to 2 visits, while severe ones need 2 to 3 visits over 3 to 4 weeks. Missed hiding points, skipped follow-ups or new entry from drains and neighbouring spaces can allow cockroaches to return.

Why does mopping too soon matter?

Treated surfaces should not be mopped for 48 hours. Mopping sooner can remove or weaken residual insecticide around skirting, entry points and drain perimeters. Re-entry is safe 2 hours after treatment, but keep the no-mopping period for the treated surfaces.

What if cockroaches return between scheduled visits?

Check whether treated surfaces were mopped within 48 hours and whether high-risk areas were missed. If you are on the bi-monthly plan, free re-treatment is included when cockroaches return between visits. Otherwise arrange an inspection to review hiding points, entry points and the method used.
